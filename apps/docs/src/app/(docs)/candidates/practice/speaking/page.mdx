# Speaking

Speaking is the one module Free cannot open.

<Callout tone="pro" title="Pro only">
  There is no per-attempt meter to run out of, the way essay marking has one on
  Free — the module itself is locked until you are Pro. Starting a speaking test
  on Free sends you to [Plans and limits](/candidates/plans-and-limits).
</Callout>

## Sitting it

A test runs the three real IELTS Speaking parts in order: Part 1 (short,
familiar questions), the long turn (a topic card, one minute to prepare, up to
two to speak), and Part 3 (a discussion that extends Part 1's topic). Each
answer is recorded in your browser and uploaded when you finish.

## How it is graded

Speaking is graded from your **audio directly** — the model listens to the
recording itself against the IELTS band descriptors, the same way an examiner
does. A transcript is produced alongside it, but only to show you what you
said and to check the report's quoted lines are verbatim; it is not what the
grader reads.

## Your report

The report shares its shape with
[your writing report](/candidates/practice/your-writing-report): an overall
band — the mean of four criteria, rounded to the nearest half band — and the
four Speaking criteria:

- Fluency and Coherence
- Lexical Resource
- Grammatical Range and Accuracy
- Pronunciation

Below that, four to eight verbatim moments pulled from what you actually said,
each one good, a grammar note, or a point you asserted without developing.
Every answer is also playable individually, with its transcript alongside it.

A speaking attempt counts toward your awards, your streak, and your Speaking
band on [Progress](/candidates/progress) — the same as any other module. It
does not currently feed [your study plan](/candidates/your-study-plan).

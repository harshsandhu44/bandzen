# The diagnostic

The diagnostic is the first measurement. Until you have taken it, the plan on
Today is a sensible default rather than yours, and Progress has nothing to draw.

## What it consists of

Two attempts, chained, in one sitting:

| Part | What                                  | Time       |
| ---- | ------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| 1    | The easiest available reading passage | 20 min     |
| 2    | A Task 2 essay                        | 40 min     |
|      | **Total**                             | **60 min** |

You are asked for a target band and a test date first, if you have not already
given them.

The two halves are recorded as linked attempts, which is why the result page can
show them as one sitting and one overall band while Progress still counts them
as a reading attempt and a writing attempt.

## Your result

Reading is marked the moment you submit it. Writing goes off to be marked, which
takes up to a minute, so the result page may show your reading band first and
fill in the essay when it lands. You can close the tab; the marking finishes
without you.

The overall figure is the mean of the two. From it, Bandzen builds
[your study plan](/candidates/your-study-plan) — and the plan is where the
diagnostic actually pays off, because it now knows which of your two skills is
behind and by how much.

## Taking it more than once

<Callout tone="pro" title="Pro">
  Free includes {FREE.diagnostics} diagnostic. Retaking is a Pro feature. If you
  have already been measured, the page offers **Retake with Pro** and a link
  back to your last result, rather than letting you start one and stopping you
  at the end.
</Callout>

Retaking is not usually the useful move anyway. A second diagnostic measures the
same two skills the same way; ordinary practice attempts feed Progress and the
plan just as well, and Reading practice is unlimited on every plan.

## Where it sits in Practice

["Sit a test"](/candidates/practice) on the Practice hub lists the diagnostic
alongside its section, duration, difficulty and whether you have taken it — next
to a dashed, locked card for the four-skill mock test, which does not exist
yet.

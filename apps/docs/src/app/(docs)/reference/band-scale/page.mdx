# The band scale

IELTS reports on a 0–9 scale in half-band steps. Bandzen estimates on the same
scale so its figures are comparable to the ones you are aiming for.

## Reading

Your raw score is scaled to 40 first, so a 13-question practice set reports on
the same scale as a full test. Then it converts:

This is the public Academic Reading conversion. It is currently applied to

## Writing

Four criteria, each given its own band by the marker:

<ul className="mt-4 list-[square] space-y-2 pl-5 text-[0.9375rem] leading-7 marker:text-chrome">
  {WRITING_CRITERIA.map((criterion) => (
    <li key={criterion}>{criterion}</li>
  ))}
</ul>

The overall band is the **mean of the four, rounded to the nearest half band**.
It is not a separate judgement made on top of them, so a report where three
criteria are 6.5 and one is 5.0 lands at 6.0 — and the 5.0 is the thing to work
on.

## Your overall estimate

Where both modules have been measured, an overall figure is the mean of them.

Two modules, not four. IELTS averages all four skills; Bandzen only has two of
them, so a Bandzen overall estimate is not directly comparable to an IELTS
overall band. It is a measure of your reading and writing.

## What a band from Bandzen is not

<Callout title="An estimate, not a score">
  It is produced by this product from work you did here. It is not an IELTS
  score, it does not come from IELTS, and it does not predict what you will get
  on the day. Writing bands in particular come from a language model reading
  against the published descriptors, and two markings of the same essay can
  differ.
</Callout>

Read the movement, not the point. One essay half a band above the last is inside
the noise. Five attempts trending upward is a signal.

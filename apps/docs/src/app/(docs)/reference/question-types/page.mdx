# Question types

Five reading question types exist in Bandzen. They are the unit the whole system
works in: your accuracy is tracked per type, your weakest type decides what
[your plan](/candidates/your-study-plan) asks for next, and lessons are indexed
against them.

## True / False / Not Given

Does the passage state this, contradict it, or neither?

The whole difficulty is the third option. **Not Given** means the passage does
not say — not that it seems unlikely, and not that you know it to be untrue from
elsewhere. Most errors here are a candidate reasoning from outside the text.

## Yes / No / Not Given

The same three-way choice, applied to the **writer's opinion or claim** rather
than to a fact.

The distinction from True/False/Not Given is what is being tested: whether the
writer thinks it, rather than whether it is so.

## Multiple choice

One option from a list. The distractors are usually built from things the
passage does say, somewhere other than where the answer is — which is why
locating the right part of the passage matters more than reading all four
options carefully.

## Matching headings

Choose the heading that matches each paragraph, from a shared list.

Two rules make it work like the real exam: the list holds **more headings than
paragraphs**, and each heading is used **at most once**. The surplus headings are
distractors, and without them the last answer would come free.

## Sentence completion

Fill a gap from the passage, usually within a word limit.

The answer is a form of words the passage uses. Both the grammar of the sentence
and the wording of the passage constrain it, which is what makes it a test of
scanning as much as understanding.

## How accuracy is used

Bandzen tracks how you do on each type across every attempt. A type needs a
handful of questions behind it before it counts — see
[Progress](/candidates/progress) — and once it does, your weakest type is what
smart practice offers you and what your plan drills.
